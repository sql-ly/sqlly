# 2026-10-05

## App Store purchases are recorded on your SQLLY account

When you buy the SQLLY Lifetime License in the Mac App Store edition while signed in, or sign in afterwards, the purchase is now recorded on your SQLLY account, so the account shows the license and your Macs count against it like any other license. The App Store purchase licenses SQLLY on your Macs only; the Account window says so, and Windows and Linux installs signed in to the same account stay on the free tier unless the account also has a license from sqlly.app. Restoring purchases, relaunching and Apple's own purchase updates all keep it in sync, and refunds Apple reports remove the license from the account again. If the purchase already belongs to a different SQLLY account, the Unlock SQLLY window says so instead of moving it.

## Signed Windows builds and a Microsoft Store package

Windows builds of the app and the SQLly Bridge can now be signed with SQLLY's verified publisher identity, so Windows names the publisher instead of warning about an unknown one, and the same signed app can be packaged for the Microsoft Store. A copy installed from the Store leaves updates to the Store, says so in About and Check for Updates, and gets its sqlly:// links and SQLite "Open with" entry from the Store package instead of writing them to the registry. Downloads from sqlly.app work as before.

## Exports keep numbers as numbers on every engine

Exporting results to Excel from a PostgreSQL, MySQL or MariaDB connection
produced a workbook where every number was a left-aligned text label that
would not sum, and Oracle NUMBER, ClickHouse nullable decimals and DuckDB
unsigned integers had the same problem. The causes were in the engine
drivers: PostgreSQL queries labelled every column as text, and MySQL
reported its wire type names (`long`, `newdecimal`) that nothing
downstream recognised. PostgreSQL results now carry their real column
types, with integers, floats and booleans arriving typed, and MySQL
columns are named the way `SHOW COLUMNS` prints them. Excel, JSON and
JSON Lines, the INSERT, temp-table, SQL VALUES, UPDATE and IN-list
scripts, and the programming-language snippets all write a numeric
column's values as numbers — decimals keep every digit they came with —
while text columns that merely look numeric (SKUs, postal codes with
leading zeros) stay text. The grid benefits too: those columns now
right-align and take the numeric formatting preferences.
