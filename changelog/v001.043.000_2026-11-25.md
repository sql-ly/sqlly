# 2026-11-25

## Procedures, functions, triggers and other DDL format cleanly

The formatter now understands routine scripts instead of treating them as one long query. Procedure, function and trigger headers get a line per part, BEGIN … END, IF / ELSE, WHILE, TRY / CATCH and CASE bodies are indented by depth, and each statement in a body sits on its own line. This covers T-SQL, PostgreSQL, MySQL (including DELIMITER scripts), Oracle and SQLite, and every script is checked at every point of being typed so Format as You Go never scrambles a half-written routine.

## Consistent keyword casing in DDL

Words such as ADD, AFTER, INSTEAD OF, FOR EACH ROW, TRUNCATE, CASCADE, sequence options and SET NOCOUNT ON were left in whatever case you typed while everything around them was cased. They now follow your keyword casing, as do data types in ALTER TABLE, and a column that merely shares one of those names keeps your spelling. Reserved words used as values, such as Oracle's SYSDATE, are cased as keywords too.

## Auto Detect recognises more T-SQL, and follows the tab's connection

With the formatter's dialect left on Auto Detect, scripts that use dbo., NVARCHAR, DATETIME2, UNIQUEIDENTIFIER, SET NOCOUNT ON, CREATE PROC or an AS BEGIN block are now read as T-SQL; before, a procedure without a parameter could come back with its END glued to the last line of the body. In the editor, a tab that is bound to a connection formats in that connection's dialect, so a half-typed statement is no longer guessed wrong.

## Suggestions that fit CREATE, ALTER and DROP statements

The suggestion list used to propose query functions almost everywhere in DDL. It now offers what belongs at each spot: object kinds after CREATE / ALTER / DROP, data types after a column or parameter name, constraint words after a type, the table's own columns in keys, indexes, CHECK and computed columns, and the right tables and columns after REFERENCES. Spots where you are naming something new stay quiet, and DEFAULT puts the usual choices (current time, new ID, NULL) first.

## Fewer out-of-place function suggestions in queries

After FROM and JOIN only functions that return rows are offered, not every scalar function. Typing @ in a procedure or function body lists its parameters and variables, including right after a comparison, and END, ELSE, WHEN and THEN are offered inside an open CASE or block.

## Enter keeps procedure and trigger bodies indented

Typing a routine by hand with Format as You Go off, Enter after a statement such as SELECT 1; dropped the next line out of the BEGIN block, a trigger's AFTER INSERT, UPDATE line pushed the body one level too deep, and END stayed at the body's depth. New lines now stay at the block's depth, END returns to its BEGIN when you press Enter, and a line after a finished statement starts where that statement did.
